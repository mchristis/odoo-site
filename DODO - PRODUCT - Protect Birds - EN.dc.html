A better place to nest

Birds are not the problem.

The warm, narrow space beneath a solar array can look like shelter while creating noise, hygiene issues and avoidable risks for eggs and young birds. DODO prevents access before a nest becomes active and helps homeowners provide a more suitable place in the garden.

Why birds choose solar panels

A sheltered space close to food.

Solar panels create shade, shelter from rain and a narrow space above the roof. Urban birds that already use ledges, cavities and roof gaps may investigate the array for nesting. The same location can also be close to food sources and warmer building surfaces.

Why the space can become unsuitable

The right moment is before nesting begins.

The underside of an array was not designed as habitat. Heat can build, exits can be difficult for young birds, and cables, rails and roof features restrict movement. Once eggs or dependent young are present, access must not simply be closed.

What homeowners notice

Common signs include repeated entry beneath the same panel edge, early-morning scratching or calling, twigs and nesting material around the roof, feathers, droppings and increased bird activity at one corner of the array.

What the installer must do first

Every DODO installation begins with a visual bird inspection. If an active nest, eggs or dependent young are present, the installer stops. The project is rescheduled or referred for country-specific advice. DODO must never be used to seal birds inside.

A better alternative

Close the wrong place. Open a better one.

After the unsuitable roof space has been closed at the correct time, DODO supports habitat in the garden. Regional flowers provide resources for insects; feeders can support birds during appropriate periods; species-specific houses can replace suitable cavity habitat. The Bird Guide explains which measures fit which species.

A better one, on us

Because we like birds: every completed DODO installation comes with a free bird feeder or birdhouse — the homeowner chooses which.
