EMON data and device security: European rules and risks

For management and R&D. Based on public sources up to 21 October 2026. Not legal advice; have counsel review before publication or contracts.

1. Summary

  • Hosting EMON data in Germany is necessary but not sufficient. Under GDPR, remote access to EU personal data from outside the EU counts as a transfer, even when the servers stay in the EU.
  • The second European concern is control: who can switch off or change inverters and batteries remotely. Lithuania already restricts this by law; the EU restricts funding and is preparing supply-chain rules.
  • For STRØHM.ENERGY, the most sensitive points are access by our own teams outside the EU, and any manufacturer cloud or firmware channel in MOEBIUS, FLUXX, SYNQ and OZZY.
  • On the website: describe design requirements, label them as proposed, and do not name countries or claim immunity.

2. Binding today

GDPR: transfers to China (TikTok decision)

On 2 May 2025 the Irish Data Protection Commission fined TikTok €530 million: €485 million for unlawful transfers of EEA user data to China (Article 46(1)) and €45 million for not informing users (Article 13(1)(f)). The decision treats remote access by staff located in China as a transfer, even when no data is copied to a Chinese server. China has no EU adequacy decision; the DPC found the safeguards did not account for Chinese laws that give authorities broad access to data, including the National Intelligence Law and the Counter-Espionage Law. The Irish High Court upheld the findings on 3 June 2026.

Lithuania: remote control of energy devices

Article 73³ of the Law on Electricity requires that solar, wind and storage installations above 100 kW are secured so that entities from countries listed as a national security threat (China is on the list) cannot remotely change output parameters or switch devices on and off. It applies to new installations from 1 May 2025 and existing ones from 1 May 2026. Grid operators may not connect installations that do not comply. Equipment is not banned; operators must add safeguards.

EU funding restriction (May 2026)

The Commission restricted EU funding, including EIB and EIF financing, for solar, wind and storage projects that use inverters from suppliers associated with high-risk countries (China, Russia, Iran and North Korea). The Commission estimated the cost impact of switching at under 2% of total project cost.

Cyber Resilience Act: own brand means manufacturer

A company that places a product on the EU market under its own name or trademark takes on the manufacturer’s obligations. A STRØHM.ENERGY-branded inverter or battery built by an OEM therefore makes STRØHM.ENERGY responsible for risk assessment, vulnerability handling and security updates over the support period.

Since 11 September 2026, actively exploited vulnerabilities and severe incidents must be reported: an early warning within 24 hours and a notification within 72 hours, followed by a final report. This is separate from GDPR breach notification.

GDPR: scope and transfers

GDPR does not require that EU data stays on EU servers; transfers are lawful with an adequacy decision or appropriate safeguards. China and Hong Kong have no EU adequacy decision. A Hong Kong company offering services to EU users can itself fall under GDPR, and servers in Germany do not make it a European company. Staying stricter than the minimum (EU-only storage and processing, no default non-EU access) is a policy choice that must be demonstrable technically and contractually.

EU Data Act

Users of connected products gain the right to access data their equipment generates in use and to share it with third parties of their choice. Not all derived analytics or trade secrets are covered. The act also covers cloud switching and safeguards against unlawful requests from non-EU governments. Closed platforms that only offer access through their own app will fit the EU market less and less.

3. Proposed, not yet law

Cybersecurity Act revision (CSA2), 20 January 2026

Introduces a trusted ICT supply-chain framework. The Commission could designate third countries posing serious and structural non-technical risks, list high-risk suppliers, and impose binding measures up to prohibitions on use and installation, with fines up to 7% of worldwide turnover. Entities established in or controlled from such a country could be barred from certain activities. Amendments discussed in September 2026 would move from country-based to individual company assessments.

Smart Energy Expert Group recommendations, October 2026

Recommends restricting PV components and software from high-risk suppliers in all segments, including plug-in systems, and classifying inverters as Class II important products under the Cyber Resilience Act (third-party certification). Named high risks: attacks on manufacturer cloud platforms, manufacturer backdoors on behalf of a state, and attacks on utility-scale plants via local networks. The report names no country and is not the Commission's official position.

For utility-scale plants the experts recommend documented, encrypted and authenticated inverter traffic, outbound connections only to approved servers in the EU or equivalent jurisdictions, and no inbound connections from outside the local network. For commercial and utility-scale installations, manufacturer-triggered automatic firmware updates should give way to controlled manual updates by qualified maintenance providers. They also warn against a split architecture where dashboards are hosted in the EU while firmware updates, signing and remote control run from high-risk jurisdictions.

4. How EU user data can reach China

  1. Manufacturer cloud. An inverter or battery that reports to its manufacturer's cloud sends data outside EMON, wherever EMON is hosted.
  2. Remote support access. Engineers outside the EU opening dashboards, logs or databases. Under the TikTok decision this is a transfer.
  3. Firmware and hidden channels. Update servers, undocumented communication modules or accounts. In May 2025 Reuters reported undocumented communication devices found in some Chinese inverters; this is not proof that all such devices contain them.
  4. Legal compulsion. Experts cited by Reuters note that Chinese companies are required by law to cooperate with intelligence agencies; the DPC relied on the same laws.

Reported incidents

November 2024, remote disabling. Reuters reported that inverters in the US and elsewhere were disabled remotely from China in a commercial dispute between Deye and Sol-Ark. The number of devices and grid impact were not established; there is no indication of a state operation. It shows that a manufacturer can keep the ability to influence equipment after sale.

December 2025, Poland. Coordinated destructive attacks hit more than thirty wind and solar farms and other energy sites. CERT Polska attributed them to a group linked to Russia’s FSB. Entry was through internet-facing VPN interfaces without multi-factor authentication. Generation was not interrupted. The lesson is about installer and maintenance access, regardless of equipment origin.

5. What this means for STRØHM.ENERGY

  • Our own structure. We have entities in Hong Kong and Shenzhen and teams in Taichung and Delhi. Any access from there to EU personal data is a transfer. Prefer EU-only access for EU data; otherwise document a transfer impact assessment and standard contractual clauses, and keep mainland China out of EU data access.
  • Keys and firmware signing. Keep firmware signing keys, update approval and remote-command authority under EU control. This is also the best answer to a company-level CSA2 assessment.
  • Device clouds. Verify that MOEBIUS and FLUXX have no direct manufacturer-cloud link; EMON should be the only cloud, with local control as fallback.
  • Certification. Plan for possible CRA Class II third-party assessment of inverters.

7. Target architecture

Two separate security domains: an EU data platform (telemetry, history, analytics, privacy) and a stricter EU control platform (authorised commands, audit logs), behind EU identity and access. A local EMON gateway runs local control and offline operation and validates commands before they reach MOEBIUS, FLUXX, SYNQ and OZZY. Offline mode must never bypass mandatory safety shutdowns, anti-islanding or lawful grid operator commands.

13. Website wording

Use: designed to, intended to, proposed, planned, regional hosting approach, controlled access, documented controls.

Avoid: kill-switch free, fully sovereign, 100% protected, GDPR certified, guaranteed offline, and any naming of countries or competitors.

Keep data hosting, remote device control and offline operation as three separate promises; EU hosting alone does not prevent a remote shutdown.

ClaimOnly after
“All European user data stays in Europe”Verification of all data flows, backups and access
“No foreign kill switch”Independent verification of all firmware and communication channels
“GDPR compliant”Legal, technical and contractual verification
“European-controlled energy management”Verification of management and operational authority

EU hosting is not the same as EU sovereignty: foreign legal authority over cloud providers, support organisations and key holders also matters. Positioning line: “Manufactured globally. Data and energy control governed locally.”
