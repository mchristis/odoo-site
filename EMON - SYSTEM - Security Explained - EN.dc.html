01The problem explainedRemote access, data and external dependencies.

An energy management system connects more than devices. It connects information about electricity consumption, generation, storage and the daily operation of homes and businesses.

Protecting that information also means protecting the systems that produce and use it.

Protecting energy data is only one part of protecting an energy system. Equally important is understanding who can access connected equipment, who can change its settings, who controls firmware updates and which functions remain available without a permanent cloud connection.

EMON is developed with regional data protection, secure device communication and operational continuity as core requirements.

The important question is not simply where a product is manufactured. It is who can access it, which commands they can send, how software updates are controlled and what happens when a remote service becomes unavailable.

How the risk arises

The device connects

An inverter or battery may communicate with a manufacturer platform to send operating data, receive settings or obtain software updates.

Remote access creates responsibility

Remote access can help installers diagnose faults and maintain equipment. If permissions are too broad, credentials are compromised or undocumented communication paths exist, that access can create a security risk.

Control must be accountable

Users and installers should understand who is allowed to access the equipment, which commands are permitted and how access is recorded.

Not every inverter has a remote shutdown capability, and not every external connection is dangerous. Risk depends on the actual hardware, firmware, network configuration and access permissions.

Media report14 May 2025
Documented hardware concerns

Reuters reported in May 2025, citing unnamed US sources, that undocumented communication hardware had been found in some Chinese manufactured inverters and batteries. The report did not establish the number of devices affected or identify the manufacturers.

Why it matters

What matters is hardware transparency and verification: knowing which connections a device actually has, not only the ports its manufacturer documents. The report does not mean that every Chinese-manufactured product is unsafe.

Reuters
Official incident report29 December 2025
Attacks on energy communications

CERT Polska reported coordinated cyberattacks on 29 December 2025 targeting more than 30 wind and photovoltaic installations, as well as other energy infrastructure. Communication with the distribution system operator was disrupted at renewable installations, although electricity generation continued.

Why it matters

Security covers communication, availability and recovery, not only whether equipment can be switched off.

European cybersecurity guidance

The European Commission published photovoltaic cybersecurity recommendations in September 2026. The report examines risks from internet connected PV installations and measures to strengthen protection.

Why it matters

Expert recommendations are not binding legal requirements. Binding obligations come from legislation such as the Cyber Resilience Act, whose reporting duties have applied since 11 September 2026.

03Explore four scenariosNormal operation, cloud outage, firmware update and remote shutdown.

Four situations, explained

What happens?

The inverter, battery and meter send measurements to the monitoring platform. Settings and energy management commands travel back to the equipment on site.

Why does it matter?

Reading data and changing how equipment operates are different activities. They carry different risks and need different permissions.

How is the risk addressed?Design objective

EMON is designed to send data and commands along separate routes, each with its own access rules. Commands are intended to be checked by the local controller before they reach a device.

See normal operation in the technical architecture
What happens?

The internet connection or a remote service becomes unavailable. Monitoring in the portal stops, remote support is not possible and cloud-based forecasts pause.

Why does it matter?

If local energy management depends on the cloud, an outage can affect battery charging, self-consumption or load control on site.

How is the risk addressed?Design objective

The local controller is designed to keep essential functions running without the cloud. Which functions continue depends on the connected equipment and its validated configuration. Tested behaviour is added per product to the Transparency record.

See cloud outage in the technical architecture
What happens?

New software is installed on a device. It changes how the device operates.

Why does it matter?

An update from the wrong source, or one that fails, can change settings, disable functions or leave a device unusable. The source, authenticity, approval, installation and recovery procedure all matter.

How is the risk addressed?Design objective

STRØHM.ENERGY firmware for MOEBIUS, FLUXX, SYNQ and OZZY is designed to be signed by STRØHM.ENERGY and installed only after approval, with a recovery route.

Firmware for third-party devices is supplied and digitally signed by its own manufacturer. Where the device allows it, EMON is designed to require approval for these updates and record them.

See firmware update in the technical architecture
What happens?

A device receives a command to stop producing or to disconnect.

Why does it matter?

A shutdown can be legitimate: for electrical safety, maintenance or a lawful instruction from the grid operator. The risk arises when an unauthorised party can send that command, or when remote access is broader than necessary. The concern is unauthorised or excessive control, not every shutdown.

How is the risk addressed?Design objective

Each command is intended to be checked before it is carried out. Necessary safety shutdowns and lawful grid operator commands are not blocked.

PermissionWhether that party may send it

04How EMON addresses the risksAccess rights, local operation, command validation and firmware.

How EMON is designed to reduce these risks

Data and control separation

Monitoring data and commands that affect equipment serve different purposes and should have distinct access policies.

TechnicalSeparate data platform and control platform; commands are designed to be checked at the local EMON controller.

See normal operation in the architecture
Command authorisation and validation

Commands should be associated with an identified and authorised party, checked against defined limits and recorded.

TechnicalIdentity, permission, scope, device safety limits and logging per command.

See remote shutdown in the architecture
Local operation when cloud connectivity is lost

Local operation should not depend unnecessarily on continuous access to a cloud service. Only functions validated for the connected equipment are described as available offline.

Validated functions are listed per product in the Transparency record as testing is completed.

TechnicalLocal automation and safety limits run on the controller.

See a cloud outage in the architecture
Firmware governance and manufacturer access

Firmware updates and maintenance access should be documented, authenticated, restricted and auditable. EMON and STRØHM.ENERGY firmware fall under STRØHM.ENERGY’s responsibility. Firmware and cloud services that a third-party manufacturer provides for its own devices remain with that manufacturer.

TechnicalSTRØHM.ENERGY firmware releases designed to be signed by STRØHM.ENERGY; third-party firmware signed by its manufacturer; manufacturer access documented per device.

See a firmware update in the architecture

These are design objectives, not verified product capabilities. Separating data and commands reduces risk. On its own, it does not protect against every hardware or firmware risk, which is why each supported device is assessed separately.

Principles in detail
European data. European infrastructure.

Our architecture is designed to keep European customer data within the European Union, with Germany as the primary hosting location.

This approach includes the protection of consumption history, system measurements, device information, backups and relevant operational records.

Regional hosting provides more than low latency. It supports privacy, data protection, transparency and greater control over the infrastructure handling European energy information.

Technical design requirements
Primary hosting in Germany, second separate EU backup location
Encryption in transit and at rest, with EU-controlled key management
EU support access by default; subprocessors documented

Privacy begins with the data itself.

Energy consumption can reveal patterns of daily life, building occupancy and equipment usage.

We believe this information should be collected only for defined purposes, protected against unauthorised access and retained only as long as necessary.

EMON is designed around privacy-conscious data collection and clearly defined access permissions.
