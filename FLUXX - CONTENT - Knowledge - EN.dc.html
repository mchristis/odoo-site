How battery storage works

Draft

A battery moves solar energy from the hours you make it to the hours you use it. EMON decides when to charge and when to discharge.

01
Charge from solar
Solar power the home doesn't use right away goes into the battery.
02
Store
The battery holds the energy until it's needed. The system keeps a reserve you set.
03
Use at night
In the evening and at night, the home runs on stored energy first.
04
Grid as backup
When the battery reaches its reserve, the grid takes over.
05
Buy low, use high
With a dynamic tariff, EMON charges the battery from the grid when the price is low and powers the home from the battery when the price is high.

Storage terms, explained

General explanations of how storage is built and connected. Not every FLUXX family carries every feature described here — check the specific product for what's confirmed.

Portable power station
A self-contained, carry-anywhere battery for temporary or off-grid power. Example families: PICO, BOLT, NODE, TORQ, DOCK, WORK.
Plug & play storage
A pre-configured system, installed but not custom-engineered. Documented connection method and installation requirements vary by country — check the product before assuming any model can connect to an ordinary socket. Example families: KUBUZ, POUF, OTTO, BENCH, TURA, BOWI.
Monobloc battery
A single fixed-capacity unit. Some monoblocs (BOWI, TRIFFID) include an inverter; others (BLOQ) are battery only and need one paired separately. Fit is confirmed by documented pairing, not by voltage match alone.
Stackable / modular battery
Built from repeatable modules, so capacity scales with the number of modules used. STAQ and KYUZ are modular; expansion limits are set per product and are not unlimited.
Cabinet & container storage
Larger commercial and C&I formats. SILO is a cabinet, KOFR is a separate all-in-one enclosure, and DOKK is the container range. Not every unit includes PCS or transformer equipment — check the specific product.
Battery only vs. complete system
A battery-only product (BLOQ, STAQ) stores energy but needs an inverter to use it. A complete system (BOWI, TRIFFID, POUF/OTTO/BENCH) already includes one.
AC-coupled vs. DC-coupled
AC-coupled storage connects on the AC side, alongside an existing solar inverter. DC-coupled storage (LINEA, KYUZ) shares the DC bus with solar before conversion — typically more efficient, but tied to a specific inverter pairing.
LV vs. HV
Low-voltage (LV) and high-voltage (HV) describe the battery's internal architecture. STAQ batteries and the MOEBIUS inverters ALVIX and LUNOX come in matching LV/HV pairs — an LV battery pairs with an LV inverter, not an HV one.
Inverter, PCS, BMS & energy management
The inverter (or PCS, in larger systems) converts between AC and DC. The BMS protects and manages the battery cells. Energy management software decides when to charge and discharge.
kWh, kW and kVA
kWh is stored energy (capacity). kVA is apparent power; kW is real power — they aren't the same and FLUXX specifications are kept in the unit as documented, not converted.
Nominal vs. usable capacity
Nominal capacity is the battery's rated size. Usable capacity — what you can actually draw — is typically lower and is confirmed per product where published.
Backup vs. grid-connected operation
Grid-connected operation optimises normal daily use. Backup capability — keeping the lights on during an outage — is a separate, confirmed-per-product feature, not assumed by default.
