Commercial administration with its supporting records

Review what was agreed, what was delivered and what is ready to invoice. Keep the commercial record connected to approved project information rather than assembling it after the work is complete.

01
Understand the project position
Compare the accepted scope with purchased materials, recorded labour and approved changes. Distinguish estimates, commitments and actual costs when reviewing the result.
02
Prepare the correct invoice
Check the billing entity, customer details, agreed milestone and supporting work. Retain deposits, variations and remaining balances in the project context.
03
Continue recurring services
Connect maintenance or other agreed services to their contract and billing schedule. Review changes before they affect the next billing period.
04
Work with your accounting process
Define where accounting records are maintained, which information is transferred and how differences are resolved. The implementation should support your accountant’s process and the requirements of each operating entity.

Does KERN include our complete accounting scope?

The scope is agreed during implementation. Confirm the accounts, countries, reporting and any external accounting connections required before deciding what is included.

Can we see estimated and actual margin separately?

Those values should use distinguishable inputs. An estimate based on planned materials and hours is different from a result based on approved actual costs.

People matched to the work required

Plan around the skills and availability needed for the assignment. Keep personnel information relevant to project delivery while restricting access to sensitive employment and cost records.

01
Maintain relevant profiles
Record the roles, skills and qualifications the planning process needs. Define who checks those records and how an expired requirement affects assignment.
02
Coordinate availability
Use agreed working patterns, leave and existing commitments when considering an appointment. Keep planned availability distinct from a confirmed commitment to the customer.
03
Include subcontractors
Assign external people only the project information and functions needed for their work. Set access duration and review it when the assignment ends.
04
Connect time with project cost
Use approved time records and the agreed cost basis to review labour against the estimate. Keep confidential rates visible only to authorised roles.

Can KERN replace payroll?

Payroll is a separate implementation decision. Personnel, planning and time records do not by themselves establish a complete payroll service.

Time recorded against the work

Connect hours to the project or task they belong to. Give the responsible reviewer the information needed to check entries before they are used in costing or other agreed processes.

Read more: Time recorded against the work
Capture the context
Record the work reference, date, duration and relevant activity. Distinguish installation, travel, preparation and other categories where your business needs that information.
Review and correct
Identify incomplete entries and explain adjustments. Keep submitted, returned and approved records recognisable as different states.
Compare with the estimate
Review actual approved hours against the planned effort. Use the difference to understand the job and improve future estimates without changing the original baseline.
Apply configured rules
Breaks, working patterns and warnings should reflect your agreed policies and applicable requirements. Confirm the scope before relying on time data for employment or payroll decisions.

Can field teams submit hours from their device?

That is part of the intended field workflow. Confirm supported entry methods and approval requirements during implementation.

Are unapproved hours treated as final costs?

They should remain distinguishable. Reporting must explain whether a value includes submitted estimates or approved actual time.

Absence visible to the planning process

Give employees a defined request process and managers a clear review step. Reflect approved absence in the availability used for project scheduling.

Read more: Absence visible to the planning process
Define the categories
Set the leave types, entitlement rules and approval responsibilities relevant to your organisation. Restrict sensitive absence details to the people who need them.
Review the request
Allow the responsible manager to consider the dates, entitlement and team coverage. Retain the decision and explain what happens if the request needs to change.
Update availability
Approved leave affects planning availability. A pending request should not be confused with an approved absence or silently alter a confirmed assignment.

Are entitlement rules the same in every country?

No. Rules depend on the employment context. The implementation must use the policy and legal requirements appropriate to the affected staff.

Access defined by responsibility

Give people access to the records and actions their work requires. Separate internal administration, assigned project work and customer-facing information.

Read more: Access defined by responsibility
Define the role
Identify which responsibilities belong to sales, planning, finance, field teams and administrators. Viewing a record, changing it and approving an action may require different permissions.
Scope the assignment
A subcontractor may need one project’s documents without access to the rest of the business. A customer may need shared invoices and appointments without internal costs.
Review access over time
Change permissions when responsibilities change. Remove access when a person leaves the organisation or an external assignment ends.
Keep operational authority separate
KERN access does not automatically grant EMON control. Device commands require the supported connection and separately authorised operational permissions.

All questions in Knowledge
Can one sign-in access several services?

A connected sign-in can simplify access where implemented, but permissions remain specific to the organisation, service and records involved.

Does hiding a screen protect its data?

No. Restrictions must be enforced for the underlying request and action. The interface should reflect those permissions rather than act as their only control.

Consistent settings for the organisation

Maintain the users, document standards and workflow settings that support your team’s work. Define which administrators may change them and how those changes are reviewed.

Read more: Consistent settings for the organisation
Manage users and roles
Add people according to their responsibility, assign the agreed access and remove it when no longer required. Keep external access distinguishable from internal administration.
Maintain templates
Use approved document and communication templates for the relevant company and workflow. Review changes before they affect new customer-facing material.
Configure working procedures
Keep project types, stages, task groups and form requirements aligned with the way the business delivers work. Update the procedure when responsibilities or products change.
Manage imports and exports
Check the data source, mapping and user authority before bulk changes. Review exceptions and retain enough information to investigate an incorrect result.
