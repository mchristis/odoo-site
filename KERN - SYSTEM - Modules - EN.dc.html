01
Leads and CRM
Capture, qualify and follow up every lead. Every lead is stored with source, owner and next action. Overdue follow-ups raise a reminder.
  • Lead capture from web forms, e-mail, phone, referrers and the mobile referral app
  • Duplicate check on address and contact
  • Lead status: new, contacted, appointment, quoted, won, lost
  • Follow-up reminders with due date per owner
  • Contact, company and installation address as separate records
  • Inactive quotes closed automatically after a set period

Customer records with project context

Keep the people, organisations and sites involved in an installation connected to their work. Open the relevant opportunity, quotation, project or service record from the same customer context.

Identify the right contact

Separate the person making the enquiry from the site owner, billing contact and project decision-maker where those roles differ. Keep contact responsibilities clear when several organisations are involved.

Connect contacts to sites

A customer may have several properties. A commercial site may involve an owner, tenant, facilities manager and EPC. Record those relationships so that the team contacts the appropriate person.

Retain the history

Keep agreed requirements, proposal references and relevant communication attached to the record. Give the assigned team enough context to continue the conversation without asking the customer to repeat it.

Control visibility

Share the information required for the user’s role. Internal pricing, personnel information and unrelated customer records remain outside the scope of ordinary project access.

Questions about CRM
All questions in Knowledge
Can one customer have several projects?

Yes. Structure the customer relationship separately from individual sites and projects so that each installation retains its own scope and history.

What happens when a prospect becomes a customer?

The existing record continues into the quotation and project workflow. Review duplicate records before creating another customer entry.

Every enquiry with an owner and next action

See which enquiries are new, which need technical information and which are waiting for a customer decision. Keep the pipeline relevant to the way your team sells and delivers work.

Qualify the requirement

Record the site, requested solution, timing and available information. Identify what is needed before the team can prepare a credible proposal.

Make progress visible

Use defined stages to distinguish an initial enquiry from a qualified opportunity, a submitted proposal and an accepted project. Record why work is paused or lost so that an inactive record does not appear ready to proceed.

Keep follow-up specific

Assign the action, responsible person and due date. A request for a roof survey is different from a pricing discussion; the next step should state what needs to happen.

Accept calculator enquiries deliberately

When a visitor shares a KALK scenario, retain its reference and assumptions with the enquiry. An indicative calculation remains subject to technical and commercial review.

Questions about lead management
All questions in Knowledge
Can different project types use different stages?

The pipeline should reflect the agreed sales processes. Solar supply, an installed battery and a multi-site charging programme may need different qualification steps.

Does every calculator user become a lead?

No. A public calculation becomes a shared enquiry when the visitor chooses to submit it and provides the information required for follow-up.

02
Site survey
Record roof, electrical and site data on location. Surveyors record site data on a tablet or phone, also without network. Data syncs to the project when online.
  • Roof surfaces: type, pitch, orientation, dimensions, obstacles
  • Electrical: grid connection 1-phase or 3-phase, main fuse, earthing resistance, distribution board photo
  • Consumption: annual day and night usage, existing devices
  • Photo upload per item, tagged to the address
  • Mandatory fields per project type; the survey cannot be submitted incomplete
  • Technical and commercial survey as separate steps when required

Field information in the project record

Use structured forms for surveys, work checks and handover. Collect the information required for the next decision while retaining the project and equipment context.

Ask the relevant questions

Organise forms around the task. A roof survey, electrical check and maintenance visit require different information. Conditional questions can keep the form relevant to the answers provided.

Attach the evidence

Add photographs, files and relevant measurements to the record. Identify the location or equipment shown so that a later reviewer can understand the evidence.

Review before acceptance

Separate a submitted form from an approved technical or customer record. An incomplete check should remain visible rather than appearing as completed work.

Share the approved output

Generate or share the agreed report with the appropriate recipients. Internal comments and customer-facing records should have separate visibility where needed.

Questions about forms
All questions in Knowledge
Can forms be used on a phone or tablet?

The field workflow is intended for those devices. Confirm supported devices and any offline requirements during implementation.

Does a signature confirm the whole installation is compliant?

A signature records the stated acceptance. The form must identify what was checked, who checked it and what remains outstanding.

  • Yield estimate per roof surface (PVGIS data)
  • Cabling, connectors and accessories added per rule set

Product information with a defined source

Maintain the article references, specifications and commercial data used by your team. Keep supplier records and published engineering data connected without treating them as interchangeable.

Identify the article

Give each item a stable reference. Map supplier article numbers, product families, variants and relevant units so that sales and purchasing select the intended item.

Control changes

Record the source and effective version of important specifications and prices. New data should not silently alter the basis of an accepted quotation.

Publish suitable data to KALK

Supply approved product characteristics and compatibility information to the calculation workflow. Public users do not need access to internal supplier discounts or project margins.

Include third-party products deliberately

KERN can be configured around validated products from other brands. A catalogue entry is not proof of an operational EMON connection or of compatibility with every other component.

Questions about catalogues
All questions in Knowledge
Can supplier data be imported?

Import scope depends on the available source, rights, format and agreed connection. Confirm fields, update behaviour and exception handling before relying on a catalogue feed.

04
Quotation and signature
Generate, version and sign the offer. The quote is generated from the BOM. Each change creates a new version; earlier versions stay readable.
  • Price build-up: articles, installation cost, file handling, inspection, discount
  • Margin rules per article group; sales price derived from purchase price
  • VAT per project: 6% renovation rate or 21% standard (BE), reverse charge (B2B)
  • Subsidy lines per region where applicable
  • Quote layout in the installer's own house style, own logo, header and footer
  • Attachments: datasheets, layout, terms of sale
  • Digital signature; signed quote converts to order
  • Quote types: purchase installation, lease or PPA

Quotations connected to the agreed scope

Prepare the equipment, installation and service proposal from a controlled set of records. Keep the calculation reference, selected products, exclusions and commercial conditions together.

Build the proposal

Combine equipment, materials, labour and agreed services. Make optional items and exclusions visible so that the customer can understand what is included in each alternative.

Retain the calculation basis

Attach the selected KALK scenario where calculations support the offer. Preserve its inputs, revision and assumptions rather than replacing the accepted result when a newer scenario is created.

Review before release

Check the responsible company, prices, discounts, applicable taxes, validity and approval requirements. Keep internal purchase costs and margins out of the customer document.

Manage revisions

Identify which proposal the customer received and which version was accepted. Changes to equipment or scope should be reviewed for technical compatibility, price and delivery impact.

Continue into delivery

Use the accepted proposal as the commercial baseline for the project. Any unresolved survey, access or engineering conditions remain visible to the delivery team.

Questions about quotations
All questions in Knowledge
Can one proposal include several technologies?

Yes. Combine the relevant equipment and services while retaining the technical basis for each part of the system. Whole-system financial results should come from a consistent calculation.

Is a KALK estimate a quotation?

No. KALK supplies a calculation and proposed configuration. KERN applies the approved commercial scope, pricing and terms to create the offer.

  • Purchase orders per supplier, with expected delivery date
  • Supplier price lists and article references
  • Goods receipt against purchase order, partial deliveries supported
  • Direct project delivery or delivery to warehouse

Purchasing from the approved requirement

Use the project materials list to identify what must be supplied. Keep supplier selection, purchase approval, expected delivery and receipt connected to the installation they support.

Separate requirement from availability

The design identifies what the project needs. Purchasing checks where it will come from, under which conditions and when it can be delivered.

Prepare supplier orders

Group the approved items by supplier and responsible company. Review article references, quantities, agreed prices and delivery instructions before release.

Follow changes and shortages

Record supplier confirmations and expected dates. A backorder or proposed substitute should trigger a review of the project schedule and, where required, technical compatibility.

Confirm receipt

Compare delivered goods with the expected order. Record shortages, damage and incorrect items so that stock and project readiness reflect what actually arrived.

Questions about purchasing
All questions in Knowledge
Can the materials list come from KALK?

Yes. Bring the selected design and proposed quantities into the project, then apply the purchasing checks and approvals required by your business.

Can a substitute be accepted without recalculation?

Only where the responsible reviewer confirms that no affected technical check needs to be repeated. Compatibility and customer scope must remain controlled.

Stock information connected to the project

Know where materials are held, what is already committed and what is expected. Keep inventory movements connected to purchasing, project allocation and returns.

Define the locations

Separate warehouses, vehicles, temporary project locations and other agreed storage points. A product being somewhere in the business does not mean it is available at the installation site.

Reserve against the requirement

Associate committed materials with the relevant project. Define when a reservation is created, changed or released and who may approve the change.

Record the movement

Retain the origin, destination, item, quantity and responsible action for stock transfers. Use product identifiers and serial or batch information where that detail is required.

Manage returns and exceptions

Review unused materials, damaged goods and customer returns through a defined process. Confirm their condition before making them available for another project.

Questions about inventory
All questions in Knowledge
Does expected stock count as available stock?

It should remain distinguishable. A confirmed supplier delivery and goods received into an approved location are different states.

Can vehicle stock be included?

Include it where vehicle movements and responsibilities form part of the agreed implementation. Define how field teams record issue, use and return.

Plan the visit around the work

Coordinate surveys, deliveries, installations and service appointments with the resources they require. Give planners a view of availability and dependencies before confirming a commitment.

Match the assignment

Consider the work type, required skills, crew size, location and expected duration. Keep qualification and access requirements attached to the assignment.

Check readiness

A free calendar slot does not mean a job is ready. Review the design, material availability, customer confirmation and site conditions alongside the proposed date.

Communicate changes

When an appointment changes, identify the affected teams and customer communications. Keep the current schedule distinguishable from an earlier arrangement.

Include service work

Plan maintenance and corrective visits with the installation context available. Link the assignment to the case and required parts so that the field team can prepare.

Questions about planning
All questions in Knowledge
Can different teams have different assignments?

Yes. A survey, electrical installation and commissioning visit can have different responsible teams and prerequisites.

Are suggested dates automatically customer commitments?

No. Distinguish an internal planning proposal from an appointment that has been checked and confirmed.

Clear actions for the people responsible

A project stage shows progress. A task identifies the work required to move it forward. Keep the action, owner, due date and supporting information together.

State the action

Use a specific instruction and link it to the relevant record. The person checking a delivery date needs the supplier order; the person approving a layout needs the current design revision.

Show what is waiting

Define completion

Record the installed equipment

Complete the required checks

Invoices connected to the agreement

Use the project’s commercial scope and approved work to prepare invoices. Keep the customer, issuing entity, payment conditions and supporting records together.

Apply the agreed billing stages

Structure billing around the accepted terms: deposits, delivery stages, completion or other agreed milestones. Review the amount already billed and the balance before issuing the next invoice.

Include approved changes

Additional work and substitutions can affect the amount due. Retain the approved variation and its relationship to the original scope.

Check before issuing

Review customer and invoice addresses, dates, applicable taxes, payment terms and document references. A prepared invoice should remain distinguishable from an issued financial document.

Follow payment status

Keep payment information connected to the invoice and investigate unmatched or disputed amounts. An issued invoice and a completed payment are different events.

Questions about invoices
All questions in Knowledge
Can invoices use fixed amounts or percentages?

Define the billing method in the commercial agreement and configure the supported stages accordingly. The invoice should reflect the agreed amount and milestone.

Are invoices created automatically when work is completed?

That depends on the agreed workflow. Automation should run only when its required approvals and data checks have been satisfied.

Billing against the correct contract period

Keep recurring charges connected to their agreement and service period. Review what will be billed and retain the record of what has already been invoiced.

Set the schedule

Define frequency, first billing date, covered period and any agreed changes. Separate the service period from the invoice date and payment due date.

Review the billing run

Use a preview or approval process where included in your implementation. Check contract status, due amounts and exceptions before releasing financial documents.

Handle exceptions

A changed agreement, cancellation, failed payment or credit requires a controlled response. Retain the reason and resulting action rather than silently changing an earlier invoice.

Connect payment processing deliberately

Payment collection depends on the selected provider, supported method and customer authorisation. Confirm the connection and reconciliation process before enabling collection.

Questions about payments
All questions in Knowledge
Does automatic collection guarantee payment?

No. Transactions can fail or be disputed. The process must include status updates, exceptions and the agreed follow-up procedure.

Can payment methods vary by customer or market?

The supported methods depend on the provider and implementation scope. Confirm availability and commercial conditions for the markets you serve.

Service work connected to the installation

Give each request a record, an owner and a next step. Keep its communication, appointments and outcome connected to the relevant customer, site and equipment.

Understand the request

Record the reported issue and the information available. Separate a customer observation from a confirmed technical fault and identify any immediate action required.

Retrieve the installation context

Open the equipment details, design reference, photographs and commissioning record associated with the request. Review earlier work before deciding the next step.

Coordinate the response

Assign the case, required parts and visit. Keep the customer informed through the agreed communication channel without exposing internal case notes.

Record the resolution

Document the work performed, any replaced equipment and the result of the required checks. Update the installed asset record where the configuration has changed.

Questions about service
All questions in Knowledge
Can an EMON alert become a service case?

Yes, where that connection and workflow are included. The case should retain the alert reference and necessary evidence, with duplicate events handled deliberately.

Does closing a ticket prove the equipment is operating normally?

Closure records the agreed service outcome. Where performance verification is required, include the relevant test or measured evidence before closure.

Recurring services with a defined scope

Keep the agreement, covered assets, service responsibilities and billing schedule connected. Make it clear what the customer has purchased and what the team is expected to deliver.

Define the service

Identify the sites and equipment covered, planned visits, response arrangements, exclusions and customer responsibilities. Commercial terms should describe the actual service commitment.

Use controlled templates

Start from approved contract wording and pricing rules. Record any agreed exception so that the delivery team does not rely on a standard service assumption that no longer applies.

Manage the lifecycle

Track the start date, term, renewal and cancellation conditions. Review a changed scope or asset list before applying it to a new period.

Connect delivery and billing

Link service visits and invoices to the active agreement. Keep subscription access, technical permissions and physical equipment operation as distinct responsibilities.

Questions about contracts
All questions in Knowledge
Can one agreement cover several sites?

Where included in the implementation, define the sites and assets individually while retaining the overall commercial agreement.

Does a missed payment give KERN control of the installation?

No. Commercial payment state does not itself grant device-control authority. Operational permissions are managed through the supported EMON arrangements.
