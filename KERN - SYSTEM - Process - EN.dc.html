Your installation business in one working structure

A customer record should remain connected to the quotation, project and service history. A materials list should remain connected to purchasing and delivery. A planned visit should give the installer the information needed to complete it.

KERN organises these relationships so that each department works from the relevant project context.

Sell
Start with the customer
Record the enquiry, identify the site and assign the next action. Keep proposals and communication attached to the opportunity so that the handover to delivery includes the agreed scope.
Prepare and deliver
Deliver the approved project
Bring together the design reference, materials, tasks, appointments and field records. Track changes against the approved baseline and make their cost and schedule impact visible.
Invoice
Close the commercial record
Review completed work, approved hours and supplied materials before invoicing. Retain the documents that explain the amount billed and the work accepted.
Service
Continue with service
Link a service request to the original installation. Give the assigned team access to the relevant equipment, photographs, commissioning records and earlier work.

The working record for each installation

Use the project to connect what was sold with what needs to be delivered. Keep its design reference, tasks, appointments, materials, documents and commercial milestones available in one context.

Define the project stagesSet the stages that reflect your delivery process, from survey and technical review to procurement, installation and acceptance. A completed stage should have a clear meaning for the team receiving the next handover.
Track dependenciesIdentify what prevents work from proceeding. Missing approval, unavailable equipment and incomplete site access are different issues and should have an owner and resolution step.
Control changesRecord the requested change, its reason and its impact. Review substitutions or additional work before they alter purchasing, scheduling or the customer’s agreement.
Preserve the installed realityAt handover, retain the final equipment mapping and accepted records. The original proposal remains part of the history, while the installed configuration becomes the reference for service.

Configure responsibilities

Different teams need different views. Set access according to the organisation, company, role and assigned work. Keep customer-facing information separate from internal costs and personnel records.

Connected where it is useful

Use KALK for calculations, EMON for supported operational functions and agreed integrations for other business services. Each connection has a defined purpose and data scope.

Questions about the process

All questions in Knowledge
Can we start with a limited scope?

Yes. Define a useful first workflow and the records it needs. Additional functions can be introduced once that workflow is established and the next stage has been agreed.

Will our team need to use several interfaces?

KERN provides its own interface for the agreed workflows. Where an external service remains part of the process, its role and access requirements are explained during implementation.

Can one programme contain several sites?

Yes. Use an overall programme view while retaining the individual scope, dates, costs and permissions of each site project.

What happens after handover?

The project record remains available to the authorised service team. Service requests can refer to the installed equipment and the original commissioning evidence.
