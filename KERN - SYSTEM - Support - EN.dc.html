Which technologies does KERN cover?
PV, battery storage, heat pumps, EV chargers and mounting systems. One project can combine several.
Do I have to use STRØHM.ENERGY products?
No. Any supplier's articles can be loaded; STRØHM.ENERGY articles arrive pre-loaded with datasheets and prices.
Can I keep my own quote layout?
Yes. Quotes, delivery notes and invoices use your logo, colours, header, footer and wording.
How long does setup take?
It depends on catalogue size and data import. Timing is fixed in the intake step.

Does the field app work without network?
Yes. Survey and handover data are stored on the device and synced when a connection returns.
How does KERN handle Belgian inspection requirements?
The handover module collects serials, photos and the single-line diagram, and plans the AREI inspection with the documents attached. The final invoice is released after the handover is complete.
Which VAT rates are supported?
Rates are set per project: 6% renovation rate and 21% standard in Belgium, other EU rates per entity, and reverse charge for B2B.
Can agents and referrers work in KERN?
Yes. They register leads through the referral app; commission is calculated per signed order.
Can I run several companies or brands?
Yes. KERN supports multiple entities, brands, currencies and numbering series in one environment.
Where is my data stored?
Data is hosted in the EU. A GDPR data processing agreement is part of every contract.

Implementation around a defined workflow

Start with the work the team needs to complete. Agree the records, responsibilities and connections required, then validate the process before expanding the scope.

1 Review the current processFollow an enquiry through quotation, delivery and service. Identify repeated entry, unclear responsibility and the decisions that need to remain controlled.
2 Define the first releaseChoose a useful scope with clear acceptance criteria. Specify which modules, users, entities, locations and connections are included.
3 Prepare the dataIdentify the records to migrate and their sources. Review duplicates, incomplete fields and the relationships needed to connect customers, sites, articles and open work.
4 Configure and testUse representative cases from your business. Test approvals, changes, exceptions and access as well as the ordinary path.
5 Train and pilotGive each role the procedures needed for its work. Use a controlled pilot to identify gaps before relying on the process more widely.
6 Release and reviewAgree the transition, support route and responsibilities after launch. Review the working process before introducing the next stage.

How long does implementation take?

Timing depends on scope, data quality, custom workflows and connections. The proposal should identify stages, dependencies and the responsibilities required from each side.

Do we have to change everything at once?

No. A staged rollout can retain selected systems while the ownership and handover between them are clearly defined.

Bring the records needed for the next stage

Migration should preserve useful business context without carrying forward avoidable errors. Agree what will be transferred, what remains archived and how the result will be checked.

Define the scopeIdentify customers, contacts, sites, products, open quotations, projects and relevant documents. Historical finance and service data may need a separate assessment.
Review the qualityCheck duplicate identities, outdated articles, missing fields and inconsistent units. Decide who resolves exceptions before import.
Map the relationshipsA document without its project or a site without its customer is difficult to use. Preserve the relationships the workflow depends on.
Reconcile before releaseCompare representative records and agreed totals with the source. Resolve exceptions and approve the transition before the migrated data becomes the working reference.

Support connected to the way you work

Give users a clear route for questions and issues. Separate a usage question, configuration change and software fault so that the right people can respond.

Train by responsibilitySales users, planners, finance staff and field teams need different procedures. Training should use representative tasks from the configured workflow.
Report useful contextIdentify the affected record, action, time and expected result. Include relevant evidence without sharing credentials or unnecessary customer information.
Track the next actionKeep the response and ownership clear while the issue is investigated. Where work cannot proceed, record the affected business process and agreed temporary approach.
Maintain the implementationChanges to users, templates, products or connections may require configuration rather than incident support. Define that responsibility within the service scope.

Is support available at all times?

Support hours, channels and response commitments must be stated in the commercial agreement. No round-the-clock service is implied by this page.

Are custom changes included?

That depends on the agreed service. New functionality and integration changes should have a clear scope and approval process.

Understand how your information is accessed

Business records involve customers, staff, prices and commercial decisions. Define who may access them, which services process them and what responsibilities apply to your implementation.

Organisation and user accessAccess should reflect the organisation, role and assigned work. Internal group administration and access for an unrelated external business require distinct boundaries.
Connected servicesIdentify the purpose and scope of each integration. Business API access and operational device permissions are separate responsibilities.
Access changesDefine onboarding, role changes and removal of access. External assignments should have an owner and a review point.
Review the actual arrangementsRequest the security and processing information applicable to your deployment. Review hosting, retention, backups, authentication, access records and incident responsibilities against the agreed service.
