Battery architecture
All sets remain LV.
Each proposed module uses 16 cells in series.
Nominal module voltage: 51,2 V.
Cell capacity: 314 Ah.
Calculated nominal module energy: 16,0768 kWh.
Additional modules are proposed in parallel, subject to equipment approval.
Sizing rule: at least 3 kWh of nominal battery capacity per kVA, rounded upward to complete modules.
Nominal capacity is not usable capacity. Not every battery, BMS and inverter combination is compatible.
Expansion depends on compatibility, available capacity, interfaces and installation conditions.
Heating and electrical limits
The design concept is an outdoor ZORBA cabinet with integrated heat-pump equipment.
Heating-water applications are assessed at 35, 45 and 55 °C. Selected winter assessment point: −15 °C outdoor temperature.
The nominal heat-pump class is not claimed at −15 °C or at 55 °C water temperature.
The electrical ceiling applies to the complete unit, including the heat pump, pumps, controls, auxiliary electric heating and battery charging.
Auxiliary heating may supplement the heat pump and must be automatically limited or switched off when necessary.
Heat-pump modulation and heating-versus-hot-water priority are configured per project, where supported.
The UK 16 A concept and the approximately 14,4 A-per-phase boundary of the 10 kVA three-phase set need particular verification. A nominal heat-pump class alone does not establish electrical suitability.
Essential-load backup is a design requirement under investigation, with circuits selected per project. Backup operation must be explicitly included in the electrical design and configuration documentation.
Applications

Renovation, new build and repeatable construction

For terraced, semi-detached and detached homes, in new build and renovation.

Renovation
Coordinate retained and new equipment, available space, electrical supply, heat distribution and connection routes. Compatibility must be assessed against the existing installation and selected configuration.
New build
Define technical space and building interfaces early so preparation can align with the construction sequence.
Repeatable construction
Use documented configurations and consistent interfaces to support repeatable project preparation.
Phased solar
The system may be prepared for a later solar phase if the selected inverter architecture and connection design support it. Later connection requires checking the actual PV inputs, MPPT range, string voltage and current, protection, cable routes and commissioning requirements.
