How CORE works

One sealed unit on the inside wall. Two openings to the outside.

CORE is a monoblock: compressor, both heat exchangers and the refrigerant circuit sit in one indoor unit. There is no outdoor unit and no refrigerant piping on site. Two openings through the outside wall let the unit draw in outdoor air and return it.

DRAFT

HeatingHeat is taken from the outdoor air drawn through the wall, raised in temperature by the compressor and released into the room.
CoolingThe cycle reverses. Heat is taken from the room and carried out with the exhaust air.
Dehumidification and fanRemoves moisture from the room air, or circulates air only.
DC inverterThe compressor runs at variable speed and matches the output to the room, instead of switching on and off.
RefrigerantR290 (propane), a natural refrigerant, factory-sealed inside the unit.
Wall openingsTwo core-drilled openings, closed on the facade with grilles. [TO CONFIRM: opening diameter and wall thickness range]

Why demand is growing

Heating is moving from fuel to electricity.

A traditional boiler burns gas or mazout whenever heat is required. CORE turns heating from a fixed fuel expense into a controllable energy load — it can draw from whichever source costs least at that moment.

Heat only where heat is needed

Room-by-room comfort, not the whole building.

A central boiler circulates heat through a large part of the building even when one room is occupied. Each CORE unit conditions its own room or open zone. Several units across a building are coordinated by EMON as one modular system.

Every room gets its own temperature, schedule, comfort priority, solar priority and grid-use limit. An unused room stays cooler while occupied rooms get the comfort they need. This zoning is often where the largest practical saving occurs.

Installed without an outdoor unit

Heating and cooling, typically installed in two to four hours.

CORE is an indoor monoblock: the complete refrigerant circuit is sealed inside the appliance. There is no separate outdoor compressor and no refrigerant pipework to install between indoor and outdoor equipment. Only the air inlet, air outlet and external grilles are visible outside.

No outdoor unit

Heating and cooling. Nothing outside.

A conventional split system requires an indoor unit plus an outdoor condenser. NENDO CORE contains the refrigeration circuit indoors. The façade is left with only two discreet ventilation grilles instead of a large outdoor fan unit, brackets, interconnecting refrigerant lines and external cabling.

Use NENDO CORE where an external condenser is restricted, unwanted or impractical: apartments, renovations, hotels, offices, architecturally sensitive façades, modular buildings, compact homes and rooms where mounting an outdoor unit at height is undesirable.

No outdoor condenser. No long refrigerant line. One compact indoor unit.

CORE with RAY

Heat and cool directly from sunlight.

The RAY solar plug-in adds an MPPT input for a dedicated photovoltaic connection to a standard CORE. The appliance has no internal battery — solar is used when available; grid, a FLUXX battery or a MOEBIUS system provides energy at other times. Three system architectures are possible.

Solar-powered cooling

Add solar. Let the sun cover the cooling load.

On sunny days, the same solar production that creates the cooling demand can help power the cooling system. With sufficient PV output, grid import for cooling can become very small during those hours. The exact share depends on irradiance, panel size, orientation, compressor demand and system losses.

With the solar plug-in, PV modules connect to a standard CORE. Solar supplies part or all of the instantaneous compressor demand before the grid is used.

Heating with PV support

Use PV directly when it is available.

In heating mode, direct solar generation can also reduce grid electricity use whenever PV is available. The benefit is typically more variable than for cooling because the highest heating demand often occurs during periods with lower solar production.

EMON energy management

You choose the comfort. EMON manages the energy behind it.

EMON combines information from CORE units, ANDO solar, MOEBIUS inverters, FLUXX batteries, SYNQ meters, the grid, dynamic prices, room temperatures and weather — then decides when each unit heats, cools, reduces output or waits. Four selectable energy modes set the priority.

Preheating and precooling

Before electricity becomes expensive, EMON can gently shift room temperature within a comfort range you approve, then reduce demand during the expensive period. Not overheating — working inside your limits.

Battery-aware operation

With FLUXX and MOEBIUS, EMON considers battery state of charge and can reserve energy for night-time heating or a grid outage — prioritising the living room and one bedroom while reducing lower-priority spaces.

From gas or mazout to flexible electric heating

A staged transition, not an immediate full replacement.

CORE does not have to replace the existing boiler on day one. It can reduce the hours the boiler runs. During spring, autumn and milder winter periods, CORE heats occupied rooms electrically; the boiler stays available for domestic hot water, very cold weather or rooms without a CORE unit.
