Where your data lives

Your energy. Your data. Your control.

An energy management system connects more than devices. It connects information about electricity consumption, generation, storage and the daily operation of homes and businesses.

Protecting that information also means protecting the systems that produce and use it.

EMON is being developed with regional data protection, secure device communication and operational continuity as core design requirements.

Proposed architectureHosting locations and security measures shown here are design requirements. They are confirmed per region before they go live.
Regional infrastructure · data regions, not office locations
European UnionGermany

Primary hosting in Germany. Target EU-only residency for EU customer data, backups and routine processing. International support access and relevant subprocessors must be covered by appropriate technical and legal safeguards.

Our EU infrastructure keeps European customer data, backups and routine processing within the European Union. External access and subprocessors are subject to documented controls.

Hosting data in the EU does not by itself prevent unauthorised commands to a device. Communication between devices and EMON is intended to be documented, authenticated and controlled, including firmware, external connections and remote commands.

Essential local energy functions are intended to keep working during an internet or cloud interruption. Cloud services add monitoring, analytics and optimisation.

One energy management architecture, designed for worldwide deployment.

The proposed EMON architecture separates energy data from operational control. Regional environments support local data protection and regulatory requirements, while on-site control is designed to maintain essential functions when cloud connectivity is unavailable.

For European installations, the architecture is intended to support EU-based data processing and controlled access to energy systems.

Across all markets, the same principles apply: secure communication, authorised commands, controlled firmware updates and clear responsibility for device access.

In the European setup, EMON is designed to keep both under European control: the data platform as well as firmware signing, updates and remote commands.

Our target architecture keeps European customer data within the European Union, with Germany as the primary hosting location.

This approach includes the protection of consumption history, system measurements, device information, backups and relevant operational records.

Regional hosting is intended to provide more than low latency. It supports privacy, data protection, transparency and greater control over the infrastructure handling European energy information.

Energy consumption can reveal patterns of daily life, building occupancy and equipment usage.

We believe this information should be collected only for defined purposes, protected against unauthorised access and retained only as long as necessary.

EMON is designed around privacy-conscious data collection and clearly defined access permissions.

Solar inverters, battery systems, energy meters and connected devices increasingly communicate with remote platforms.

These connections provide useful functions, but they can also introduce cybersecurity risks.

Our objective is to establish documented, authenticated and controlled communication between devices and the EMON platform, with particular attention to firmware, external connections and remote commands.

Energy equipment must be protected against undocumented access routes and unauthorised remote shutdown commands.

We aim to minimise unnecessary remote-control dependencies and ensure that commands affecting energy equipment follow clearly defined security and authorisation procedures.

Necessary electrical safety functions, grid protection and legally authorised control mechanisms must remain intact.

An interruption of internet access or a remote cloud service should not automatically interrupt essential local energy functions.

EMON's intended architecture separates local protective and control functions from cloud-dependent monitoring, analytics and optimisation.

The precise offline capabilities will be defined and validated for each supported device and system configuration.

Customers should be able to understand who can access their system information, what data is collected and where it is processed.

Our development requirements include controlled installer access, secure software updates, vulnerability management and access to relevant energy data.

The objective is an energy management platform that remains transparent, maintainable and adaptable throughout its operational lifetime.
Proposed permission model

Who controls your system?

There are legitimate reasons to switch off an inverter, such as electrical safety, a grid fault or a lawful command from the grid operator. What matters is who has the authority.

This proposed permission model requires validation for each product and applicable national grid code.

The owner is intended to manage normal energy settings, user permissions and daily operation. Mandatory protective functions, such as electrical safety and grid protection, cannot be overridden.

Installation and maintenance access is intended to be time-limited, granted by the owner and logged. Access ends when the agreed period ends.

EMON is intended to optimise and control energy flows only within limits set by the owner, the device and applicable safety requirements. The local controller checks commands before they reach a device.

Legitimate grid-support, protection and control commands remain possible where law and grid code allow them. What is permitted varies by country and installation.

This is a proposed architecture and procurement requirement. Remote-control and firmware dependencies of each manufacturer are evaluated and verified per product.

The goal is to prevent uncontrolled commands from a manufacturer cloud, including remote service access and firmware dependencies. Implementation depends on the verified capabilities of each device.

Intended safeguards include layered authentication, access control, secure communication and vulnerability management. No connected system is free of cybersecurity risk.
