 02 / PRODUCT PAGE INTRODUCTION
## Solar panels and mounting. Designed together.
 LINA combines vertically positioned solar panels with an integrated support structure for rooftop installations. Its modular layout allows the solar array to be planned around the building, including roof edges, skylights, ventilation equipment, drainage and maintenance routes.
 For conventional flat roofs and vegetated roofs, LINA offers a different way to arrange solar generation: low rows of upright panels, with the spaces between them treated as part of the roof design.

 04 / APPLICATIONS
## Configure LINA around your roof
## Conventional flat roofs
 For buildings with membrane or other suitable flat roof constructions. The assessment considers roof condition, insulation, allowable loading and the compatibility of materials in contact with the roof surface. The support arrangement must protect the roof while transferring loads correctly.
## Green roofs
 For projects combining solar generation with rooftop vegetation. Panel clearance, plant height, substrate depth and access for vegetation maintenance are considered together. The solar layout should preserve access to drainage and allow the planted roof to be maintained throughout its life.
## Gravel roofs and sites requiring additional clearance
 Gravel, vegetation and local snow conditions can affect the required height beneath the panels. These applications need a specific review of the support configuration, surface condition and structural design.
 Suitability depends on the building and the approved system configuration. Roof type alone does not establish compatibility.
 05 / ENERGY GENERATION
## Consider when your roof produces electricity
 A vertical bifacial array can produce a different daily generation profile from a conventional tilted array. With panel faces directed east and west, generation can be distributed towards the morning and afternoon. The actual profile depends on location, season, orientation and shading.
 That makes the hourly generation pattern an important part of the design. For a building with daytime activity, compare expected production with actual demand before deciding the array size or whether storage is useful.
## Reflected light is part of the calculation
 Bifacial panels can use light reaching either side, including light reflected from the roof and surrounding surfaces. Roof reflectivity, panel clearance and row spacing all influence this contribution. A light roof, a planted roof and a dark roof should not be assigned the same assumed yield.
 Annual output should be estimated for the actual site and proposed layout. There is no universal percentage gain that applies to every roof.
 06 / ROOF DESIGN
## The roof remains part of the system
## Weight and local pressure
 Assess both the overall load on the building and the pressure beneath individual supports. Average kilograms per square metre do not describe the complete structural requirement. Roof insulation, support contact areas and any additional restraint or ballast also matter.
## Wind and exposed locations
 Building height, roof geometry, surrounding terrain and the position of the array affect wind loading. Any claim that a project needs no ballast or mechanical attachment must follow the assessment for that building.
## Drainage and maintenance access
 Keep drainage outlets, inspection points and equipment access clear. The layout should account for the tasks that will still be necessary after the solar installation is complete.
## Existing roof condition
 For a retrofit, assess the remaining service life of the roof before installation. Where refurbishment is approaching, coordinate roof works and solar installation as one project.

 08 / ELECTRICAL INTEGRATION
## Match the array to the electrical system
 The inverter must be selected for the final module specification and string arrangement. Compatibility depends on voltage, current, operating conditions and the inverter input limits, not simply on the total installed wattage.
 The electrical design needs to account for cold conditions, operating temperatures, bifacial current contribution and differences in shade or orientation across the roof. Cable routes, protective equipment and bonding arrangements must be coordinated with the installation.
 A battery is optional. Its value depends on when the building uses electricity and how much solar production is available for storage. Battery and energy management compatibility must be established at system level.

 10 / DELIVERY AND CARE
## Plan delivery before installation
 Confirm site access, unloading equipment, storage space and the method of moving the equipment onto the roof. Pallet dimensions and weights must be checked against the actual order. Do not assume that a roof can support concentrated pallet loads because it can support the distributed solar array.
## Routine inspection
 Include the solar system in the building maintenance plan. Inspect panels, supports and visible cabling, keep roof drains accessible and check for vegetation or debris affecting the array.
## Cleaning and vegetation management
 A vertical arrangement does not eliminate the need for maintenance. Cleaning requirements depend on local conditions. Follow the product instructions and maintain vegetation so that it does not obstruct the panels, electrical components or required access routes.
## Warranty and documentation
 Review the warranty for the exact supplied model before ordering. Product coverage, module performance coverage, mounting components and installation workmanship may have different terms and responsible parties.
 11 / FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS
## Before you choose LINA
## Is this only a mounting structure?
 The LINA concept brings the solar panels and their supporting structure together. The quotation defines the complete supplied unit and any separate accessories.
## Can LINA be installed on an existing green roof?
 A retrofit may be possible following assessment of the roof structure, vegetation, drainage and access. Existing greenery does not by itself confirm suitability.
## Does the system need ballast or roof penetrations?
 This must be established for the selected product and building. Wind conditions, roof characteristics and the approved layout determine the required restraint.
## How many units will fit on my roof?
 The usable array area is smaller than the total roof area. Roof edges, equipment, walkways, drainage and shade all affect the unit count. Use a dimensioned layout rather than dividing the roof area by a unit footprint.
## Will vertical panels produce more energy?
 That depends on the comparison and the site. Compare annual energy per installed kilowatt, energy per usable square metre and the time of production. A better result on one measure does not automatically mean a better result on all three.
## Will snow or dirt affect output?
 Vertical positioning changes how deposits accumulate, but snow, dirt and shading can still affect generation. Snow on the roof can also reduce clearance around the array.
## Can I use my existing inverter?
 Possibly, following a compatibility check using the final LINA electrical data and the existing inverter specification. Additional capacity and a suitable input arrangement must be available.
## Can I install it myself?
 Rooftop installation and electrical work need appropriate skills and equipment. Use qualified professionals and follow the applicable installation requirements.
## Is a battery included?
 Only if it is expressly listed in the quotation. Storage is selected separately according to the complete system design and the building’s energy use.
## What information is needed for a quotation?
 Start with the installation address, approximate roof dimensions, roof type, building height and photographs. Structural information, drawings and electricity consumption data help establish a more detailed proposal.
